## 5. Reading a Used Equipment Inventory

Preserved shop record for canadianmillequipment.com. The former plant is not quoting from this domain. The cart page behind the sections above reads like shorthand to anyone outside the trade, and this closing note stays inside the shop's language to explain how such a list is read. Every line item is a machine identified by its working dimensions and its maker: a 10 inch by 36 inch Ukiah double arbor gang, a 12 inch by 25 inch Roots high pressure blower, a 24 inch by 32 inch Jeffrey hog, a 54 inch McDonough linebar resaw, a 6 foot Kockums CanCar twin bandmill. The numbers are not decoration. Arbor size, feeder length, blower diameter, and motor horsepower determine what log sizes a line can accept, how fast material moves, and what the finished boards look like. A buyer reading this inventory is mentally assembling a mill: log yard and handling first, then primary and secondary breakdown, then planer and reman operations, with conveyors, screens, chippers, and sorters carrying material between stations. The about page confirms that this assembly work was the business. The company handled individual pieces through entire lines and complete mills, and worked with the client to assemble rebuilt components alongside new equipment at prices substantially less than new. That phrase, rebuilt components alongside new equipment, is the economic heart of the record. A rebuilt bandmill or a reconditioned edger only makes sense when someone who knows the trade has judged the castings, bearings, and wear surfaces still sound. The archive therefore preserves not just a list of machines but a way of buying: specify the line, source the pieces, rebuild what can be rebuilt, and spend new money only where new is necessary. For anyone assembling a mill on a used-equipment budget, the preserved inventory is the working model: read the dimensions as capacities, match the pieces into a line, and let rebuilt machinery carry the cost down only where the wear surfaces have actually been judged sound.
